System Introduction

Context

The Folger Shakespeare Library was founded in 1932 by Henry Clay Folger and his wife, Emily Jordan Folger. The Folger Library is home to the largest collection of Shakespeare materials. The institution slowly expanded from just a simple collection of materials to "a world-class research center," as well as a community center for outreach, exhibitions, lectures, and publications. The Folger Shakespeare Library states that they seek to be a place of discovery, curiosity, participation, and creativity through their mission of (1) making their collection accessible, (2) advancing understanding and appreciation of Shakespeare’s writings and the culture of the early modern world, and (3) preserving and enhancing their collection.

Users

The Folger Shakespeare Library is aimed at all people with an interest in Shakespeare and his works. The Library offers a multitude of services and experiences for people of all ages and levels of interest.

Content

According to the Folger Library's 'Folgerpedia' the collection contains approximately 277,000 total printed books, approx. 10,000 editions of Shakespeare, including 2,250 editions in languages other than English, approx. 60,000 manuscripts, approx. 90,000 graphic materials (prints, drawings, photographs, paintings), and approx. 250,000 playbills. The Folger Library also includes a wide range of electronic resources from catalogs to databases that are free to use with an account. The library also offers a subscription service for Folger researchers to gain access to additional resources.

Discussion

Interesting Features

The Folger Shakespeare Library offers a multitude of interesting and unique services, features, and experiences including but not limited to:

  • Online Box Office for Ticket Sales, Subscriptions, Group Packages, Discounts, and More
  • Free Downloadable Resources for Education Providers and Schools
  • In-person facilities and experiences including tours, exhibitions, and theatre performances
  • Opportunities for professional development, fellowships, other student resources
  • Researcher registration and reference services
  • Online catalogs, databases, and other digital resources
